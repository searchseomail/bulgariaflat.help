I am currently looking into purchasing a new build apartment in Bulgaria as an investment and potential holiday home. I've heard mixed reviews regarding construction quality and the reliability of developers in the current market. Could anyone share their experience with the buying process, specifically regarding Act 16, legal due diligence, and hidden maintenance costs? Any advice on what to look out for during the viewing phase would be greatly appreciated.

Always ensure the building has Act 16 before finalizing anything. Without it, you are technically living in a construction site, which means no legal electricity or water rates, and you cannot get home insurance easily. Check out this guide on home insurance in Bulgaria to understand your protection options.

I learned the hard way that 'maintenance fees' are often underestimated. Developers might promise low fees initially, but once the management company takes over, they skyrocket. Always ask to see the contract with the building management company before signing.

Don't trust the developer's word on 'luxury' finishes. Bring a professional property surveyor with you to the viewing. They can spot dampness, poor insulation, or subpar plumbing that you might miss in the excitement of a new build.

Legal due diligence is non-negotiable. Hire an independent lawyer who has no ties to the real estate agency or the developer. They need to check the 'Akt za sobstvenost' (deed of ownership) and ensure there are no mortgages or pending legal disputes on the land.

Many people forget to ask about the 'common parts' percentage. In Bulgaria, the square footage on paper often includes a portion of the hallway, stairwells, and lift area, which can be up to 15-20% of the total size you are paying for.
