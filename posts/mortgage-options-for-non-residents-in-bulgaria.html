Mortgage options for non-residents in Bulgaria?

MysticRaven
Topic Starter
Posted: 06.01.2025 06:04
I am currently looking to purchase a property in Sofia but I am unsure about the financing options available to non-residents. Does anyone know if Bulgarian banks typically lend to foreigners, and what the standard down payment requirements are? I have a stable income outside the EU, but I've heard that some banks might be hesitant to offer loans to non-citizens. Any advice or personal experiences with specific banks would be extremely helpful for my planning.

ShadowStriker
Replied: 24.01.2025 07:47
I've looked into this recently. Most Bulgarian banks like UniCredit Bulbank and DSK do lend to non-residents, but they are much stricter if your income is from outside the EU. Expect a lot of paperwork and a longer approval process.
CyberSage99
Replied: 27.01.2025 15:57
Standard down payment for foreigners is usually around 30% to 40%. They rarely offer the 80-90% LTV (Loan-to-Value) that locals get. You'll need to show at least 6-12 months of bank statements and proof of tax payments.

Don't forget that you'll need to translate and legalize all your income documents, including tax returns and payslips, into Bulgarian. This can add up in costs and time, so start the translations early.

The process usually takes about 2-3 months from application to payout. Make sure your credit history in your home country is spotless, as they will almost certainly ask for an official credit report from your local authority.

I strongly suggest hiring a mortgage broker who specializes in expats. They know which specific branch managers are more 'open' to non-EU applications. It saved me a lot of headaches when I was buying in Vitosha district.
CyberSage99
Replied: 13.11.2025 16:15
Banks will require an independent appraisal of the property. Be prepared for the bank's valuer to price the property lower than the actual asking price, which means you might need even more cash for the down payment than originally planned.
SolarFlare
Replied: 29.11.2025 16:22
Some banks might require you to have a residence permit (lichna karta) even if it's just a temporary one, while others are fine with just a passport and a local tax ID. It varies wildly from bank to bank.
EmeraldEye
Replied: 05.12.2025 09:06
If you're buying a 'new build' (still at Act 14 or 15 stage), banks are very hesitant to lend to non-residents until it reaches Act 16 (full completion). Stick to finished buildings if you need a mortgage to close the deal.
NeonPanda
Replied: 07.12.2025 12:46
KBC (formerly Raiffeisen) used to be quite flexible, but since their recent mergers, their criteria for non-EU income have tightened significantly. Definitely shop around and don't take the first 'no' as a final answer.
IronGrit
Replied: 10.12.2025 12:02
Make sure your debt-to-income ratio is below 30-40%. Bulgarian banks are quite conservative and will calculate your disposable income very strictly after accounting for your current rent or mortgage back home.
