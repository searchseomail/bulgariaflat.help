Buying property in Bulgaria after Brexit: What has changed for UK citizens?

IronGrit
Topic Starter
Posted: 29.01.2025 05:19
I am a UK citizen looking to purchase a holiday home in the Bansko area, but I am confused about how my status has changed since Brexit. Are there any new restrictions on property ownership for non-EU nationals, or do I need to follow a different legal process now compared to a few years ago? I am particularly concerned about the 90/180-day rule and whether it affects my ability to use the property freely. Any advice or recent experiences from fellow Brits would be greatly appreciated.

Welcome to the club! Buying property in Bansko is still very much open to UK citizens. You don't need to be an EU national to own property, but remember that you can only own the building itself, not the land underneath it, unless you set up a limited company. Before you commit, you should check out this guide on whether a lawyer is mandatory for purchasing property in Bulgaria, as it will save you a lot of stress.
EmberEdge
Replied: 07.02.2025 15:30
The 90/180-day rule is the biggest change. As a non-EU citizen, you are treated like any other tourist from outside the Schengen area. You can spend 90 days in any 180-day period in Bulgaria. If you plan to stay longer, you would need to look into a long-term residency visa, which is a completely different bureaucratic process.

Don't worry too much about the purchase process itself; it remains quite straightforward for Brits. The real challenge is the visa rule. I've found that 90 days is plenty for a holiday home, but if you want to retire there, you'll need to apply for a D-type visa based on retirement or investment.
SonicBoom
Replied: 24.03.2025 13:16
Everything is the same regarding property rights. You can buy as a private individual, but you won't own the land. Many Brits choose to register a Bulgarian company to bypass this, but that comes with ongoing accounting costs.

I've been going to Bansko for years. Since Brexit, the border control is definitely stricter, and they do stamp your passport every single time. Make sure you keep track of your days, as the fines for overstaying are not worth the trouble.
GoldenFalcon

Buying is easy, but maintaining is the tricky part. If you ever plan to rent your place out to cover costs, make sure you understand the local tax implications. It's a bit of a headache if you don't have a good accountant.
CrimsonNova
Replied: 06.07.2025 15:37
The legal process for the purchase hasn't changed, but the post-purchase administration is more tedious. You have to register your property with the local municipality and the tax office within a certain timeframe, and they are much more pedantic with non-EU passport holders now.
RadiantRain
Replied: 18.09.2025 05:53
Just a heads up: check your passport validity! Since Brexit, Bulgaria strictly enforces the rule that your passport must have been issued less than 10 years ago and be valid for at least three months after your planned departure date. Don't get caught out at the airport.

Honestly, the property market in Bansko is still very active. Most sellers are used to dealing with UK buyers, so they will guide you through the process. Just don't skip the due diligence phase, regardless of how 'easy' the agent says it is.
