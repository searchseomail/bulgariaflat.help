Can a foreign-owned Bulgarian company purchase agricultural land?

PaperPlane
Topic Starter
Posted: 02.01.2025 04:50
I am a non-EU citizen looking to invest in real estate and agricultural land in Bulgaria. I have been advised that registering a local company is the best way to facilitate these property acquisitions. Could someone walk me through the legal requirements for company registration for this specific purpose and mention any potential restrictions on land ownership for foreign-controlled entities? Any advice on reliable local accounting firms or legal services would be greatly appreciated.

Yes, you can own land through a Bulgarian company, but be aware that the company itself must be registered in Bulgaria. As a non-EU citizen, you should check out this guide on buying property in Bulgaria as a non-EU citizen to understand the initial hurdles.
UrbanNomad
Replied: 12.02.2025 04:27
Registering an OOD (Limited Liability Company) is the standard path. It is relatively straightforward, but you will need a local lawyer to handle the commercial register filing. Do not attempt this alone.
UrbanNomad
Replied: 31.05.2025 15:17
I went through this process last year. Make sure your company's articles of association specifically include agricultural activities. Also, consider reading up on the essential documents for property purchase in Bulgaria before you start signing anything.
SilentStorm
Replied: 11.06.2025 05:17
Be careful with agricultural land. Even if you have a Bulgarian company, there are strict regulations regarding who can farm the land. Sometimes the company is allowed to buy it, but you might face tax implications if you aren't an active farmer.
SilverSling
Replied: 16.06.2025 14:43
You should definitely consult with a firm that specializes in real estate law. Many general accounting firms won't know the nuances of the Agricultural Land Ownership and Use Act.
UrbanNomad
Replied: 04.08.2025 16:03
Is the land for personal use or investment? If you are looking to generate rental income, you might find that residential property is much easier to manage than agricultural plots.
SilverSling
Replied: 05.09.2025 14:59
Don't forget that as a non-EU citizen, you might also have to deal with banking compliance. Bulgarian banks are very strict about the 'know your customer' process for foreign-owned entities.
CosmicCactus
Replied: 13.10.2025 06:46
I recommend looking for law firms in Sofia that have experience with foreign investors. Avoid the 'cheap' online registration services; you need someone who understands the land law specifically.
ZenMaster

Agricultural land is a different beast compared to apartments. You might need to prove that your company intends to use the land for actual agricultural production to avoid certain restrictions.
SolarFlare

Registration is the easy part. The challenge is the due diligence on the land itself. Ensure you check for any existing land lease agreements that might be tied to the property for several years.
MysticRaven

Many people think that buying through a company solves all problems, but it adds layers of accounting work. Make sure you have a good accountant who knows how to handle the annual reporting for a company with physical assets.
