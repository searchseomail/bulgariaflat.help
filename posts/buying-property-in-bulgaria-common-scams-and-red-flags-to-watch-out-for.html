Buying Property in Bulgaria: Common Scams and Red Flags to Watch Out For

BlueHorizon
Topic Starter
Posted: 25.01.2025 13:06
I am currently looking at several coastal apartments in Varna, but I've heard some horror stories about title deed fraud and 'double selling' in the Bulgarian market. Does anyone have experience with developers asking for large cash deposits before the Act 16 certificate is issued? I want to know what legal precautions I should take and how to verify if a property has any hidden liens or outstanding debts. Any advice on finding a reliable independent lawyer who isn't affiliated with the agency would also be greatly appreciated.

EmberEdge
Replied: 02.04.2025 05:11
Never pay a large deposit in cash, especially before Act 16. If they insist on cash, walk away immediately. It is a massive red flag for money laundering or tax evasion.
InfiniteLoop
Replied: 19.04.2025 07:10
You absolutely need a lawyer who is not recommended by the agency. I found mine through a local bar association directory rather than asking the realtor for a 'friendly' contact.
PixelViper
Replied: 23.04.2025 00:53
Check the Property Register (Imoten Registar) for any encumbrances like mortgages or attachments. Your lawyer should do this, but you can request a certificate of encumbrances (tezhesti) yourself.

PixelViper

Double selling is rare but happens with off-plan properties. Ensure the preliminary contract is notarized or at least drafted by a lawyer who specializes in real estate litigation.
FrostByte_24
Replied: 04.09.2025 12:24
Always demand to see the original title deed. If the developer is still building, ask for the construction permit and the proof of ownership of the land itself.
MidnightRider
Replied: 16.09.2025 01:20
If you are a foreigner, the process is slightly more complex. You should read up on buying property in Bulgaria as a non-EU citizen to ensure you aren't missing any legal requirements.
GoldenFalcon
Replied: 08.10.2025 13:56
Be very wary of 'Act 15' properties. Many developers promise Act 16 'in a few months' but leave you stuck without utility connections for years. Never pay the full amount before the final act.
MistWalker
Replied: 23.11.2025 11:42
My advice: find a lawyer in Varna who has no ties to the developer. Check the lawyer's credentials on the official Bulgarian Bar Association website.
PixelViper
Replied: 30.11.2025 10:17
Large cash deposits are illegal in Bulgaria for transactions over 10,000 BGN. If they ask for cash, they are breaking the law, which should tell you everything you need to know about their integrity.
ZenMaster
Replied: 03.12.2025 12:50
Verify the developer's history. Look up the specific company in the Commercial Register (Targovski Registar) to see if they have been involved in insolvency proceedings.
