Clarification needed on notary fees for property purchase in Bulgaria

ZenMaster
Topic Starter
Posted: 01.01.2025 11:59
I am currently in the process of purchasing an apartment in Varna and have received conflicting information regarding the official notary fees. Could someone explain how these fees are calculated based on the property's material interest and if there are additional local taxes I should be prepared for? I want to ensure I have enough liquidity set aside for the closing process without any surprise costs at the notary's office. Any advice on how to verify these amounts before the final deed signing would be greatly appreciated.

Replies (15)

ThunderBolt
Replied: 06.02.2025 02:19
Notary fees in Bulgaria are strictly regulated by the Notary Tariff based on the 'material interest' (the property price). You can use an online notary fee calculator to get a rough estimate, but always add 20% VAT to the base fee.
HyperLink
Replied: 05.03.2025 05:46
Don't forget that besides notary fees, you need to pay a local tax, which varies by municipality—in Varna, it is usually between 2.5% and 3%. It is best to ask your lawyer for the exact figure for your specific district.
TechWiz88
Replied: 02.04.2025 11:05
I went through this last year. Make sure you ask the notary for a preliminary calculation (pre-calculation) a few days before the signing. They are obligated to provide this upon request.

Replied: 16.05.2025 16:17
The notary fee is calculated on a sliding scale. If you are a non-EU citizen, the process can be more complex, so I recommend checking Buying property in Bulgaria as a non-EU citizen - legal advice needed before you finalize your budget.
StaticShock
Replied: 18.06.2025 13:59
Always confirm if the 'material interest' is based on the tax valuation (danuchna otsenka) or the actual purchase price. Usually, the notary takes the higher of the two, which catches many buyers off guard.

Local taxes are paid directly to the municipality. Your notary or lawyer should handle the filing, but ensure you have the cash ready because these are usually due on the day of the deed signing.

The notary fee is capped at a certain amount for very expensive properties, but for most apartments in Varna, the standard percentage applies. Don't forget that you also need to pay for the notary's assistant work if they draft the deed.

I suggest setting aside an extra 5% of the purchase price to cover all closing costs: notary fees, local tax, registration, and potential legal fees. It is better to have surplus cash than to run short during the signing.

You can verify the official fees by looking at the 'Tariff for Notary Fees' published in the State Gazette. Any reputable notary will be happy to show you which bracket your purchase falls into.

Remember that the local tax is calculated on the higher value between the purchase price and the tax assessment. Always ask the seller for the latest tax assessment document to calculate this accurately.
