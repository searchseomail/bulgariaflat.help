Is Bulgaria still the most affordable country in the EU for expats?

DigitalDreamer
Topic Starter
Posted: 09.02.2025 00:38
I am currently considering relocating to Bulgaria for remote work and am trying to gauge how far my salary will go compared to living in Western Europe. While I have read that rent and utilities are significantly cheaper, I am curious if the rising costs of groceries and services are narrowing the gap with countries like Portugal or Greece. Does anyone have a realistic monthly budget breakdown for a single person living in Sofia versus a mid-sized EU city? I would appreciate any insights on whether the low cost of living is still a major advantage today.

PixelPerfect
Replied: 10.02.2025 12:37
I've lived in Sofia for three years, and while grocery prices have definitely climbed, they are still about 20-30% lower than in Portugal. Rent is where you save the most; you can still find a modern one-bedroom in a good neighborhood for €500-600, which is unthinkable in most Western European cities.

The gap is narrowing, but Bulgaria remains the cheapest option in the EU. Your biggest expenses will be rent and electricity in the winter. Don't expect the dirt-cheap prices of 2015. Eating out has become pricier, but a mid-range dinner for two is still roughly €30-40. For a single person, a realistic budget in Sofia is around €1,200-€1,500 for a comfortable lifestyle including leisure.

I moved from Greece to Sofia last year. While the grocery bill is similar, the tax rate and housing costs make a huge difference in my monthly savings. You get a much higher standard of living here on a standard remote tech salary.
