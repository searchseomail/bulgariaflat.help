Advice needed: Buying commercial property in Bulgaria for a small business

InfiniteLoop
Topic Starter
Posted: 17.02.2025 05:21
I am currently looking to purchase a commercial space in Sofia to establish a new retail outlet and office base. Does anyone have experience with the legal due diligence process for foreign buyers in Bulgaria? I am particularly concerned about checking for hidden liens or zoning restrictions before committing to a purchase. Any recommendations on reliable local law firms or common pitfalls to avoid during the closing process would be greatly appreciated.

Replies (10)

DataDrifter
Replied: 15.05.2025 05:43
The most important step is to hire a local attorney who specializes in commercial real estate. They will perform a 'Property Certificate' check at the Registry Agency to uncover any mortgages, liens, or ongoing litigation. Never skip this, even if the seller seems trustworthy.

Replied: 02.06.2025 05:16
Check out this guide on buying property in Bulgaria as a non-EU citizen if you aren't an EU national, as the process for commercial assets can be slightly more complex regarding entity registration.
MysticRaven
Replied: 01.08.2025 07:15
Zoning is a major pitfall. Ensure you visit the local municipality (Obshtina) in Sofia to verify the 'Detailed Urban Plan' for the specific area. A space might look like a perfect retail unit but could be legally designated for residential use only, which will make getting a business license a nightmare.
PaperPlane
Replied: 01.08.2025 16:25
Don't rely on the seller's documentation alone. Always commission an independent technical audit of the building's structural integrity and utility connections, especially in older buildings in the center of Sofia.
MysticMango
Replied: 21.08.2025 05:44
If you need help navigating the market, there are some great recommendations for reliable real estate agencies in Bulgaria for expats that can often connect you with English-speaking legal counsel to handle the due diligence.
IronGrit
Replied: 06.11.2025 02:35
Make sure the 'Preliminary Contract' is drafted by your own lawyer, not the seller's. It should explicitly state the penalty for the seller if they fail to clear any existing debts or encumbrances by the final deed signing date.
CrimsonNova
Replied: 29.11.2025 12:39
Be wary of 'under-the-table' price agreements. Everything should be declared at the actual purchase price in the Title Deed to avoid future tax issues or complications if you ever decide to sell the property later.
InfiniteLoop
Replied: 02.12.2025 01:18
Check if the property is part of a condominium (etazhna sobstvenost). If it is, you need to verify if there are any outstanding maintenance fees or disputes with neighbors, as these can legally encumber the property in certain situations.
WildCard
Replied: 21.01.2026 00:36
Always ensure that the person signing the contract is the actual owner according to the Property Register. I’ve seen cases where people try to sell properties using outdated Power of Attorney documents. Demand a fresh certificate from the Registry Agency dated no more than 3-5 days prior to signing.
SonicBoom
Replied: 05.02.2026 12:43
Budget for the notary fees and local taxes early on. They are calculated based on the higher value between the tax appraisal and the purchase price, and they can catch you off guard if you aren't prepared for the additional 3-5% in closing costs.
