Is a Bulgarian bank account mandatory for purchasing property as a foreigner?

SonicBoom
Topic Starter
Posted: 04.04.2025 10:09
I am currently looking to invest in real estate in Bulgaria and have found a property I would like to purchase. My real estate agent mentioned that I need to open a local bank account to facilitate the transaction and pay the utility bills later. Is it strictly necessary to have a Bulgarian account, or can I complete the purchase using an international wire transfer? Any advice on the documentation required for a non-resident would be greatly appreciated.

Replies (12)

MistWalker
Replied: 11.05.2025 09:27
It is not strictly mandatory by law to have a Bulgarian bank account to buy the property, but it is highly recommended. Many sellers and notaries prefer local transfers to avoid currency fluctuations and delays. If you are looking at new developments, check out this guide on buying a new build apartment in Bulgaria for more context on the transaction process.
CrimsonNova
Replied: 16.06.2025 13:53
You can technically wire funds internationally, but the fees and exchange rates will eat into your budget. Most people open an account once they arrive. Also, make sure you have legal representation; read more about why is a lawyer mandatory for purchasing property in Bulgaria to ensure your title deeds are clean.
MistWalker
Replied: 28.06.2025 02:11
I bought my place last year. I used an international transfer for the deposit, but opened a local account for the final balance and utilities. It makes life so much easier for setting up direct debits for electricity and water.
WanderLust_X
Replied: 15.07.2025 03:43
Opening a bank account as a non-resident can be quite bureaucratic. You will likely need your passport, a copy of the preliminary contract, and sometimes proof of address. Be prepared for a lot of paperwork.
DataDrifter
Replied: 18.07.2025 03:15
Don't bother with a local account if you only visit once a year. You can pay your utility bills through local payment services like EasyPay without needing a bank account. It’s very common for foreigners.
ThunderBolt
Replied: 06.10.2025 16:03
Many banks in Bulgaria are hesitant to open accounts for non-residents unless you have a clear purpose. Bring your purchase contract to the bank as proof. It is definitely worth the hassle for long-term ownership.
QuantumQuill
Replied: 13.10.2025 11:45
If you are looking at very cheap properties, consider the maintenance costs. Before you commit, look into whether is buying cheap property in Bulgaria actually worth it in the long run, as utility management can be tricky without a local account.
NeonNight
Replied: 27.11.2025 06:18
I used a currency broker for my international transfer to save on bank fees. The seller was fine with it as long as the full amount arrived in BGN (Bulgarian Lev) on the day of the signing.
RogueSignal
Replied: 09.01.2026 07:30
The notary is the one who will dictate the payment method. Most will insist on a bank transfer to a local account to prove the source of funds and ensure compliance with anti-money laundering laws.
LunarEcho
Replied: 10.01.2026 09:29
I've found that having a local account is essential for managing a property remotely. Paying bills via international wire every month is a nightmare due to the high transaction fees.
EmberEdge
Replied: 16.01.2026 12:07
Some banks require a local tax number or a registered address in Bulgaria. It’s often easier to hire a local accountant or property manager to handle the utility payments on your behalf if you don't want to open an account.
SilverSling
Replied: 25.01.2026 08:40
It depends on the bank. Some branches are very foreigner-friendly, while others will turn you away immediately. I suggest going to one of the larger international banks like UniCredit or Raiffeisen; they are usually more familiar with non-resident applications.
