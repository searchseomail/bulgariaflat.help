Property taxes in Bulgaria explained: What are the annual costs?

BlueHorizon
Topic Starter
Posted: 08.03.2025 05:27
I am currently looking at purchasing a holiday home in the Burgas region and want to understand the long-term tax implications. Could someone explain how the annual property tax and garbage collection fees are calculated for foreign owners? I have heard that rates can vary significantly between different municipalities like Varna or Sofia. Are there any specific deadlines I should be aware of to avoid late payment penalties?

Replies (12)

CobaltCrush
Replied: 15.05.2025 15:25
The annual costs are split into two: the property tax (danak sgradi) and the garbage collection fee (taksa smet). In the Burgas region, these are calculated based on the tax valuation of the property, which is usually significantly lower than the actual market price.
BlueHorizon
Replied: 06.07.2025 11:48
The rates definitely vary by location. For example, Sofia and Varna often have different municipal multipliers compared to Burgas. You should check the specific local ordinance for the Burgas municipality as they set their own percentages every year.
WanderLust_X
Replied: 07.07.2025 07:55
Important deadline: if you pay the full amount for the year by March 31st, you get a 5% discount. Otherwise, you can pay in two equal installments: the first by June 30th and the second by October 31st. Missing these leads to interest penalties.
PixelViper
Replied: 09.07.2025 04:39
Foreigners pay exactly the same tax rates as Bulgarian citizens. There is no special 'foreigner tax' for owning property. Just ensure your property is registered in the local tax office (Obshtina) within two months of the purchase to avoid initial fines.

Many people worry about hidden costs, but taxes here are actually some of the lowest in the EU. If you're wondering is it safe to buy real estate in Bulgaria right now?, the consistency and transparency of the municipal tax system is generally considered a big plus for investors.
SolarFlare
Replied: 27.11.2025 12:08
In the Burgas region, the garbage fee is often higher than the actual property tax itself. For a standard 1-bedroom apartment, expect to pay anywhere between 60 to 150 BGN per year total, depending on the exact tax valuation and the specific village or city zone.
PixelViper
Replied: 05.12.2025 03:59
You can pay these taxes online if you have your property's account number or a foreigner's personal number (LNCH). Most municipalities now have online portals, or you can use the EasyPay system which is available in almost every supermarket.
ThunderBolt
Replied: 10.12.2025 01:48
The tax valuation (danachna otsenka) is the base for the calculation. This document is issued by the municipality and revised periodically. It is usually much lower than the price you see on real estate listings.
VelvetVortex
Replied: 20.12.2025 10:00
The penalties for late payment are based on the statutory interest rate. While it is not a huge amount of money for a single year, it can add up if left unpaid for a long time, and you won't be able to sell the property later without a 'clean' tax certificate.
EmeraldEye
Replied: 25.12.2025 06:14
Note that if the property is located within a gated complex, there might be an additional private maintenance fee paid to the management company. This is completely separate from the municipal property taxes you pay to the government.
