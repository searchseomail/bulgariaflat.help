Buying property in Bulgaria as a non-EU citizen - legal advice needed

IronGrit
Topic Starter
Posted: 16.01.2025 16:31
I am currently looking into purchasing a holiday home in the Burgas region, but I am unsure about the specific legal steps for foreigners. Does a foreign individual need to register a company to own the land, or is that only for specific types of property? I am also curious about the average closing costs and how to find a trustworthy lawyer to handle the title deed transfer. Any advice from expats who have successfully bought property there recently would be very helpful.

Replies (12)

BlueHorizon
Replied: 01.03.2025 11:06
As a non-EU citizen, you can own the building itself in your own name, but you cannot own the land it sits on. To own the plot, you must register a Bulgarian Limited Liability Company (OOD or EOOD). It's a standard procedure and usually costs around 300-500 EUR in legal fees.
DigitalDreamer
Replied: 03.03.2025 04:20
I bought a place near Sozopol last year. Definitely hire an independent lawyer—never use the one recommended by the developer or the real estate agent. You need someone who represents only your interests to check for existing mortgages or liens.
DigitalDreamer
Replied: 13.03.2025 13:04
Closing costs typically range from 3% to 5% of the purchase price. This includes the local tax (which varies by municipality), notary fees, and the entry fee at the Registry Agency. Budget for a bit more if you are setting up a company too.
RadiantRain
Replied: 20.05.2025 15:02
The 'Act 16' is the most important document you need to look for. It's the final building permit that confirms the property is fit for habitation. Don't be tempted by 'Act 14' or 'Act 15' properties unless you really know what you are doing, as they might lack electricity or water connections.
HyperLink
Replied: 17.07.2025 03:05
For finding a lawyer, I'd suggest looking at the lists provided by the UK or US embassies in Sofia. They usually list English-speaking legal professionals who specialize in property law and have been vetted to some degree.
MidnightRider
Replied: 30.07.2025 12:00
Just a heads up: even if you buy through a company, you still need to file annual tax returns for that company, even if it has no activity. It's a small yearly cost (around 100-150 EUR for an accountant), but don't forget it!
DigitalDreamer
Replied: 25.08.2025 08:38
Burgas is a great choice! We live in the Sarafovo district. One thing to watch out for in the holiday home market is the 'maintenance fee' in gated complexes. Some can be quite high, so ask to see the maintenance contract before you sign anything.
SolarFlare
Replied: 26.08.2025 11:02
The process is actually quite fast once you have the company set up. The notary transfer takes about an hour, and then it takes a few days to get the final registration documents back. Just make sure your funds are in a Bulgarian bank account ready for the transfer.
SilentStorm
Replied: 10.09.2025 16:14
If you're a non-EU citizen, the company route is the only legal way to get the land. Most people use their home address in Bulgaria as the company's registered office. It sounds complicated, but any decent lawyer handles this for you as a package deal.
GoldenFalcon
Replied: 12.12.2025 03:17
Be very careful with 'bargains' in the rural areas around Burgas. Sometimes the ownership history is messy with multiple heirs. Your lawyer must perform a full 10-year history check on the title deed.
AlphaOmega
Replied: 23.12.2025 11:39
I'm from the UK and bought recently. The biggest shock for me was that the 'notary price' and the 'actual price' can sometimes be different in the documents to save on taxes. I strongly advise against this—declare the full price to protect yourself legally.
RadiantRain
Replied: 26.12.2025 03:56
Make sure you factor in the exchange rate. Property prices are usually quoted in Euro, but the official transaction at the notary must be recorded in Bulgarian Lev (BGN). The bank fees for large transfers can also add up.
