Is Airbnb investment in Bulgaria actually profitable in 2024?

PixelViper
Topic Starter
Posted: 03.01.2025 10:52
I am considering purchasing an apartment in Sofia or near the Black Sea coast specifically for short-term rentals. I am trying to figure out if the current market saturation and management fees make this a viable long-term investment. Has anyone here had success with Airbnb hosting in Bulgaria, or are the seasonal fluctuations too risky for a steady income? I would appreciate any insights on tax implications or property management challenges for foreign investors.

Sofia is a year-round market, but the competition is fierce. You need a unique interior design to stand out from the sea of generic listings. Management companies usually take 20-25%, which eats into your margin significantly.
GhostProtocol
Replied: 04.02.2025 15:35
I own two units in Varna. The summer season is great, but the winters are dead quiet. If you are looking for steady income, stick to Sofia, but read up on Sunny Beach market trends before putting your money into coastal property.
AlphaOmega
Replied: 14.03.2025 11:43
Tax-wise, as a foreign investor, you will pay a 10% flat tax on rental income. Make sure you register as a sole trader or company to deduct expenses properly, or you will end up paying more than necessary.
AlphaOmega
Replied: 11.04.2025 09:35
Management challenges are real. Finding a reliable cleaning crew that actually shows up on time is the hardest part of the business. Don't rely on remote management unless you have a trusted local partner.
UrbanNomad
Replied: 18.04.2025 09:16
The current market saturation in Sofia is high, especially in the city center. Many people are pivoting to mid-term rentals for expats to avoid the constant turnover and cleaning costs associated with Airbnb.
MysticMango
Replied: 16.06.2025 07:36
Before you commit to a purchase, you should definitely check if buying off-plan property is a better route for you, as new builds often have better energy efficiency and modern amenities that guests prefer.
EmeraldEye
Replied: 23.06.2025 06:01
I've been hosting in Plovdiv for three years. It's much more stable than the Black Sea coast. The seasonal fluctuation on the coast is brutal; you make all your money in July and August and then spend it trying to keep the place maintained during the off-season.
CosmicCactus
Replied: 12.08.2025 06:39
Don't forget to account for the 'tourist tax' and the mandatory registration of guests with the local police. It's a bureaucratic nightmare if you don't have a local accountant helping you stay compliant.
RadiantRain
Replied: 19.08.2025 04:22
Is it profitable? Yes, but only if you buy below market value. If you pay premium prices for a renovated apartment in Lozenets, your ROI will likely hover around 4-5% after all expenses and management fees.
MistWalker
Replied: 21.08.2025 02:21
The biggest risk right now is the potential for new regulations on short-term rentals in Sofia. The city council has been discussing stricter rules for Airbnb-style listings, so keep an eye on that legislation before you sign any purchase agreement.
TechWiz88
Replied: 29.08.2025 03:30
I'd suggest looking at secondary cities or university towns if you want less saturation. Sofia is great for business travelers, but the acquisition costs are getting quite high compared to the rental yields.
