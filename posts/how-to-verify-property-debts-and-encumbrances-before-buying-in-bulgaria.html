How to verify property debts and encumbrances before buying in Bulgaria?

CyberSage99
Topic Starter
Posted: 16.01.2025 13:27
I am currently in the process of purchasing a residential apartment in Sofia and want to ensure there are no hidden legal issues or outstanding debts tied to the title. Could someone explain the process of obtaining a Certificate of Encumbrances from the Registry Agency and what exactly I should look for in the document? Are there any other specific records I should check to confirm that the seller has paid all utility bills and maintenance fees? I want to make sure I am fully protected before signing the preliminary contract.

Replies (13)

MysticRaven
Replied: 03.03.2025 16:07
The Certificate of Encumbrances (Udozestoverenie za tezhesti) is mandatory. You can request it from the Registry Agency (Agentzia po vpisvaniyata) via a notary or directly online. It will show mortgages, foreclosures, or pending litigation against the property.
MysticRaven
Replied: 11.03.2025 09:48
Always hire an independent lawyer to perform a 'due diligence' check. They will check the property history back 10 years to ensure there are no hidden claims or inheritance issues that the Registry Agency might miss.
GhostProtocol
Replied: 20.03.2025 10:49
Regarding utilities, ask the seller for the 'EGN' or customer numbers for electricity (CEZ/Electrohold), water (Sofiyska Voda), and heating (Toplofikatsiya). You can visit their offices with the seller to request a 'no debt' certificate (bezzagovorno udozestoverenie).
AlphaOmega
Replied: 08.05.2025 14:00
Don't forget maintenance fees! Ask for a document signed by the building manager or the homeowners' association (Etazhna sobstvenost) proving that all monthly maintenance fees are paid up to date.

If you are looking for long-term investment potential, you might be interested in researching rental yields in Bulgaria, but ensure the title is clean first before calculating any ROI.
StaticShock
Replied: 27.06.2025 09:21
The Registry Agency certificate is just the start. Make sure the notary verifies the 'Akt 16' (Usage Permit) for the building, otherwise, you might face issues with utility contracts later.
ZenMaster
Replied: 02.07.2025 11:34
Check for 'vazbrani' (foreclosures). If the seller is in debt, the property might be seized by a private enforcement agent (Chastniy sudieben izpulnitel). This will clearly appear on the Certificate of Encumbrances.

DigitalDreamer

I strongly advise against paying any deposit before the lawyer has confirmed the Certificate of Encumbrances is clean. Never trust the seller's word that the property is 'clear'.
MysticMango

The 'Notary' you choose to handle the final purchase will also do their own check, but don't rely solely on them. Do your own verification beforehand to avoid losing your deposit if issues arise.
GravityZero
Replied: 09.11.2025 03:25
Check the 'Cadastral Map' (Kadastar) as well. Sometimes the physical boundaries or the square footage in the title deed don't match the official cadastral records, which can cause massive headaches during the transfer.
PixelViper
Replied: 11.12.2025 04:47
Ask the seller for a 'Certificate of No Tax Liabilities' (Udozestoverenie za lipsa na zadalzheniya) issued by the local municipality (Obshtina). This proves they have paid all property taxes.
StaticShock
Replied: 17.12.2025 14:45
If the seller is a company, check the Commercial Register (Targovski registar) to see if the company is in insolvency proceedings, as that would put the property at risk of being clawed back by creditors.
