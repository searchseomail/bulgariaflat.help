Selling a property in Bulgaria while living abroad: What are the necessary steps?

ShadowStriker
Topic Starter
Posted: 06.01.2025 07:30
I own an apartment in Sunny Beach but moved back to the UK several years ago and no longer have the time to visit. I am looking to sell the property, but I am unsure how to handle the legal paperwork and power of attorney from outside the country. Do I need to fly back to Bulgaria to finalize the sale, or can this be managed entirely through a local lawyer? Any advice on the current tax implications for non-residents would also be greatly appreciated.

Replies (14)

QuantumQuill
Replied: 09.01.2025 06:31
You definitely don't need to fly back. I sold my place in Bansko while living in London. You just need to hire a local lawyer and grant them a Power of Attorney (PoA). It needs to be notarized and likely apostilled in the UK before being sent to Bulgaria.
NeonPanda
Replied: 29.04.2025 12:14
The process is quite standard for non-residents. Your lawyer will draft the PoA, you get it signed at a notary in the UK, and then it's mailed over. Make sure you check the latest Property taxes in Bulgaria explained: What are the annual costs? so you don't have any outstanding debts that could block the sale.
EmeraldEye
Replied: 24.05.2025 12:43
I used a specialized property agency in Sunny Beach that handled everything from A to Z. They have an in-house legal team that deals with the PoA and the deed transfer. It saved me a lot of stress.
PixelViper
Replied: 20.06.2025 09:48
Don't worry about traveling. A Power of Attorney is the standard route. Just ensure your lawyer is reputable. Since you've been away for a while, double-check your 'Tax Certificate' status, as the notary will require proof that all municipal taxes are paid.

The tax situation is straightforward. As a non-resident, you generally pay a 10% tax on the capital gain, but if you've owned the property for a certain period, there might be exemptions. Consult a tax accountant familiar with the UK-Bulgaria tax treaty.

I had to get my PoA signed at a notary in the UK and then sent to the Bulgarian embassy for legalization. It adds a few weeks to the timeline, so factor that into your planning.

You don't need to be there in person. Your lawyer can represent you at the notary in Bulgaria during the final signing. Just make sure the PoA is very specific about the property details to avoid any hiccups.

Is it safe to sell remotely? Yes, provided you hire a lawyer who is independent of the buyer's agent. Always keep your own legal counsel to protect your interests.
