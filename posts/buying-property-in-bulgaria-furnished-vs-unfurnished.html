Buying Property in Bulgaria: Furnished vs. Unfurnished?

UrbanNomad
Topic Starter
Posted: 06.01.2025 08:32
I am currently looking at apartments in Sofia and Varna and I am torn between buying a fully furnished place versus an empty one. While the furnished options are move-in ready and save me the hassle of logistics, I'm worried about the quality of the furniture and whether it will match my personal style. On the other hand, starting from scratch with an unfurnished unit allows for total customization, but I'm concerned about the time and effort required to source everything from abroad. What has been your experience with the durability of furniture in Bulgarian listings, and is it worth the premium price?

Replies (9)

AlphaOmega
Replied: 04.02.2025 14:41
I bought a furnished place in Varna three years ago. Honestly, the furniture was 'rental grade'—cheap particle board that started sagging within months. If you plan to live there long-term, buy unfurnished and invest in quality local craftsmanship.
NeonPanda
Replied: 15.04.2025 01:15
It depends on your timeline. If you are just thinking of moving to Bulgaria, start with a furnished rental for six months. It gives you time to explore the furniture markets in Sofia without the pressure of a permanent purchase.
ShadowStriker
Replied: 22.04.2025 10:35
I've found that 'furnished' in Bulgaria usually means the seller just didn't want to deal with moving their old stuff. Don't pay a premium for it unless it's high-end designer gear. Most of it ends up in the bin.
EmeraldEye
Replied: 26.05.2025 12:38
I went the unfurnished route in Sofia. It was a headache for a few weeks, but I sourced everything from local manufacturers who actually build solid wood furniture. It cost less than the 'furnished' premium and looks way better.
MidnightRider
Replied: 29.05.2025 00:19
Don't underestimate the logistics. Setting up a home from abroad is a full-time job. If you buy unfurnished, make sure you budget for someone to manage the deliveries, especially regarding monthly utility expenses for a standard 2-bedroom apartment in Sofia as you'll need those accounts set up while you're fitting the place out.
DigitalDreamer
Replied: 06.06.2025 05:57
I bought furnished because I needed to hit the ground running for work. The durability is hit or miss. My sofa was terrible, but the kitchen cabinets were custom-built and excellent. Inspect the hardware closely before you agree to the price.
SilverSling
Replied: 24.08.2025 11:03
If you buy unfurnished, look into 'BGN' retail chains. They have decent modern options that won't break the bank. Buying an empty unit is almost always better for resale value because you aren't stuck with someone else's aesthetic choices.
NeonNight
Replied: 29.10.2025 14:37
My advice? Negotiate the furniture out of the deal. Tell the seller you'll pay a lower price if they clear the unit. Most sellers are happy to skip the cost of hiring a moving company to take their old junk away.
SonicBoom
Replied: 23.01.2026 09:51
It really comes down to whether you want a 'turnkey' solution or a home. If you want a home, go empty. If you just want a place to crash while you figure out your life in Bulgaria, buy furnished but expect to replace everything piece by piece over the first year.
