Is buying an apartment in Bansko still a viable investment for winter rentals?

PaperPlane
Topic Starter
Posted: 15.02.2025 10:59
I am considering purchasing a studio or one-bedroom apartment in Bansko specifically to rent out during the peak ski season. I've heard mixed reviews about management fees and the saturation of the rental market in recent years. Could anyone share their experience with current occupancy rates and the actual net return after accounting for maintenance and service charges? I'd appreciate any advice on which neighborhoods offer the best balance between proximity to the gondola and long-term value.

Replies (15)

VelvetVortex
Replied: 17.02.2025 01:27
Bansko is definitely a buyer's market right now, but be careful with the maintenance fees. Some complexes charge exorbitant rates that eat up 30-40% of your rental income. Always check the hidden costs of buying property in Bulgaria before signing anything.
MistWalker
Replied: 19.03.2025 05:22
I've owned a studio near the gondola for five years. Occupancy is great from December to March, but the summer months are dead. You really need to calculate your net return based on only 4 months of heavy usage.
PixelViper
Replied: 04.04.2025 04:32
The 'Gondola area' is the only place that holds value. Anything further out requires a shuttle service, which guests hate. Stick to the Glazne street area if you want repeat bookings.
GhostProtocol
Replied: 07.04.2025 01:58
Management fees are the killer here. If you don't manage it yourself via Airbnb, you'll barely break even after the agency takes their 20% cut plus the building's maintenance fees.
DigitalDreamer
Replied: 07.04.2025 09:05
Consider looking at the big picture of Bulgarian real estate. Some people ask if investing in Sunny Beach real estate is still a smart move in 2024, but Bansko offers a different kind of predictable, seasonal yield.
GoldenFalcon
Replied: 15.07.2025 01:11
I bought a one-bedroom last year. My net return is about 4% after all expenses. It's not a gold mine, but it's a decent hedge against inflation if you find a well-run complex.
IronGrit
Replied: 21.07.2025 13:40
Avoid the older complexes with poor insulation. Heating costs in Bansko are brutal, and if your apartment isn't energy-efficient, your rental profit will vanish into electricity bills.
PixelViper
Replied: 03.09.2025 16:04
Market saturation is real. There are thousands of apartments in Bansko. To succeed, your unit needs to be renovated and have high-speed internet, which many older units lack.
DigitalDreamer
Replied: 12.10.2025 01:09
Don't expect to make a fortune. Bansko is a lifestyle investment. If you want to use the apartment yourself for a few weeks a year, it makes sense; as a pure cash-flow play, it's tough.
ZenMaster
Replied: 27.10.2025 15:13
Have you looked into financing? Some people find that mortgage options for non-residents in Bulgaria are quite limited, so be prepared to pay cash.
EmeraldEye
Replied: 17.11.2025 01:54
The best neighborhoods are near the Kempinski or the main gondola station. Guests want to walk to the slopes in their boots. Anything requiring a 15-minute walk will be hard to rent at a premium.
NeonNight
Replied: 25.12.2025 06:21
I've been renting mine out for three seasons now. Occupancy is 90% in Jan/Feb, but drops to 30% in March. Factor that into your projections.
HyperLink
Replied: 27.12.2025 06:29
Check the building's 'Common Areas' maintenance budget. Some complexes in Bansko are poorly managed, and the elevators or pools break down constantly, which leads to bad reviews and lower occupancy.
LunarEcho
Replied: 01.01.2026 07:09
If you buy, don't rely on the complex's in-house rental management. They are often inefficient. Hire a local private property manager who actually cares about your ratings.
CobaltCrush

Bansko is changing. It's becoming a hub for digital nomads year-round, not just skiers. If you can target the remote worker crowd, you might get better occupancy outside of winter.
